Case Study — Home Care

Modernizing At-Home Care Operations With AI

A national home-care provider needed to scale delivery ahead of a major reimbursement policy shift — without growing headcount.

Industry Home & aged care
Engagement 8 weeks
Scope 22 care managers, 74 care workers, 4 operational teams
Outcome ~25% more client-facing time (projected)

The constraint wasn't the workforce. It was the work surrounding them.

An American aged care provider was growing. But the way work was getting done was becoming harder to scale.

More than 22 care managers and 74 care workers were serving hundreds of clients, while administrative work was taking an increasing share of their time.

A single assessment could generate hours of documentation. Between 80% and 90% of referrals arrived missing critical information. More than 100 shift notes had to be reviewed every day. Meanwhile, families wanted more proactive communication about schedules, reviews, and changes to care.

At the same time, sector reform was putting further pressure on margins.

The organization didn't need another AI demo. It needed to know where AI could remove real operational friction.

We found 16 opportunities. Then we narrowed them to five.

Over eight weeks, Quelx interviewed more than 30 team leaders and frontline staff, assessed four operational teams, mapped workflows, and tested potential solutions against the reality of how the work was actually being done.

We identified 16 opportunities. Five were worth pursuing.

That distinction matters. A long list of AI use cases isn't a strategy. The value comes from knowing which problems are worth solving, which aren't, and why.

Knowing what not to build is part of the strategy.

What we prioritized

The roadmap focused on the parts of the operation where administrative work was consuming capacity or slowing decisions.

We looked at how assessments were documented, how incomplete referrals were handled, how large volumes of care notes were reviewed, how safety concerns could be surfaced earlier, and how clients and families could receive more timely information.

These weren't speculative AI ideas. Each opportunity was connected to an existing workflow, validated with the people doing the work, and assessed against implementation effort and expected return.

The opportunity

Our recommendations identified the potential to increase client-facing time by approximately 25% by reducing the documentation burden around assessments.

That's the point.

The value of AI isn't how many models an organization deploys. It's what the organization can do with the capacity those systems give back.

More time with clients.

More attention to risk.

More proactive communication.

More room to grow without adding administrative overhead at the same rate.

Eight weeks. Five priorities. A clear path forward.

Before the engagement, the organization knew AI could help. What it didn't know was where AI would matter most.

Eight weeks later, leadership had a validated roadmap with five prioritized solutions, implementation estimates, and projected ROI — something they could take to the board and act on.

We didn't give them more AI ideas. We showed them what was worth building.
